In February 2022, Jensen Huang addressed analysts with the vocabulary of a diversified technology executive—gaming, automotive, professional visualization, Omniverse. Twenty months later, after ChatGPT rewired global expectations, the same man spoke to the same audience with measurably different language. Not expanded. Compressed.

This analysis examines three NVIDIA quarterly earnings calls spanning February 2022 through November 2024, extracting only Huang's speaking portions. The corpus totals 10,906 words across three time points: pre-ChatGPT baseline (Q4 FY2022, 3,954 words), ChatGPT inflection (Q4 FY2023, 3,553 words), and Blackwell era (Q3 FY2025, 3,399 words). Primary metrics are lexical diversity (unique tokens divided by total) and Shannon entropy (base-2 logarithm measuring information density).

The trajectory defies expectation. Most executives facing explosive domain growth expand vocabulary to capture new territory. Huang contracted. Lexical diversity fell from 0.234 to 0.218, a 6.8% decline. Shannon entropy compressed from 8.22 to 7.94 bits, down 3.5%. Yet technical density—AI-specific terminology as proportion of total speech—increased 37.9%, from 4.91% to 6.77%. The vocabulary underwent wholesale replacement rather than accretion. "Scaling" appears zero times in the early corpus and 47 times per 10,000 words by 2024. "Inference" follows identical trajectory: absent in 2022, 56 occurrences per 10,000 by late period. Meanwhile "cloud" collapsed from 33 to under 9. The lexicon rotated.

The compression reveals not institutional capture but evangelical focus. Where Cook's diversity declined as Apple matured into operational optimization, Huang's declined because he stopped explaining NVIDIA's portfolio and started explaining a single paradigm shift. The generalist vocabulary of 2022—digital twins, ray tracing, autonomous vehicles, professional workstations—gave way to concentrated repetition orbiting one gravitational center. By November 2024, he speaks of "AI factories" manufacturing "digital intelligence" the way earlier generations spoke of electrical utilities. He found his metaphor and hammers it.

The decline in linguistic diversity accompanied by rising technical density reveals something specific about executive communication during technological discontinuity. Huang's vocabulary compressed not because institutional forces constrained him—NVIDIA faced no existential regulatory threat—but because he discovered a frame so powerful it obviated discussing anything else. This is the linguistic signature of genuine conviction, distinguishable from institutional capture by what replaces the lost diversity. His newer vocabulary includes "agentic," "reasoning," "tokens," "sovereign AI," "physical AI," "test-time scaling"—the terminology of an emerging paradigm, not bureaucratic hedging. The hapax ratio remained stable at 50% across all periods, indicating consistent novel terminology even as aggregate diversity fell. Compression trending toward precision rather than away from it.

If Huang's compression reflects paradigm focus rather than vocabulary exhaustion, his linguistic diversity should rebound when a new discontinuity emerges—quantum integration, biological interfaces, or some unforeseen frontier. A CEO whose compression reflects institutional capture shows monotonic decline; one whose compression reflects evangelical focus shows periodic expansion as new territories demand new language. Monitor for lexical diversity exceeding 0.24 at any future call as signal of paradigm boundary crossing. The measurements suggest that sometimes compression is not the cage closing but the lens focusing.
