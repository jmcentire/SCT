Mary Barra's Shannon entropy declined 3.9% over a decade of leading General Motors while her hedge-to-assertion ratio dropped 22%. The vocabulary expanded slightly; the information density compressed. More words carrying less meaning per word. This is the linguistic signature of convergence toward confident assertion of the institutional script—a form of compression the Cage thesis predicts but that manifests differently than defensive hedging.

This analysis examines Barra's earnings call transcripts across two periods: early tenure (Q1, Q2, and Q4 2014 plus June 2014 stockholders meeting; 4,750 words) during the ignition switch recall crisis, and recent tenure (Q1-Q4 2023, Q1-Q3 2024; 3,291 words) as GM navigates the EV transition. Primary metrics are lexical diversity, Shannon entropy, and hedge-to-assertion ratio.

The early-tenure baseline captures a CEO under extraordinary pressure. Barra had been in the role for months when the ignition switch defect became public, forcing recalls of 29 million vehicles and congressional testimony about deaths the company had failed to prevent. Lexical diversity registered 0.249; Shannon entropy 8.526 bits; hedge-to-assertion ratio 0.156. She named specific consequences—"removed 15 employees," "60 safety investigators," "280 suggestions"—and hedged around forward commitments. Crisis demanded operational precision and strategic caution simultaneously.

The recent-tenure data shows the drift. Lexical diversity rose to 0.268, a 7.8% expansion. Shannon entropy fell to 8.193 bits, a 3.9% decline. Hedge-to-assertion ratio dropped to 0.122. The vocabulary around EV strategy, capital allocation, and Cruise grew more confident even as targets retreated: "well-positioned," "building momentum," "on track," "strong results." The language of achievement replaced the language of intention—but achievement described in increasingly repetitive terms.

Barra holds no supervoting shares and no founder authority. She arrived as a professional manager with standard fiduciary exposure. The board can remove her; shareholders can pressure the board. The governance structure provides no insulation from institutional constraint. The linguistic trajectory reflects this exposure: convergence toward the phrases that satisfy analyst expectations, board requirements, and legal review. The confidence is real; what it's confident about is the approved narrative.

The Cage thesis predicts compression toward defensible mean. Defensive hedging is one mode; assertive recitation of the institutional script is another. Both represent reduced degrees of freedom. Barra's entropy decline alongside her assertion increase indicates vocabulary narrowing toward a smaller set of high-frequency phrases deployed with greater confidence. The palette shrinks while the strokes grow bolder. She says "record results" and "strong execution" and "well-positioned" with increasing frequency and decreasing qualification. The variance that characterizes exploratory thinking has been replaced by fluent repetition of what's expected.

The prediction follows from the pattern identified: Barra's assertive register should remain stable while targets continue retreating. Watch for entropy to continue declining as the approved vocabulary ossifies. Watch for hedge-to-assertion ratio to spike only if operational crisis returns—the 2014 data proves she possesses the hedged register when circumstances demand it. Absent crisis, the institutional script will continue to assert itself confidently.
