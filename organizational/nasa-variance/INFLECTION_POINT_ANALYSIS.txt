HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION
-------------------------

The linguistic shift aligns perfectly with the Apollo-to-Shuttle transition:

1969: Apollo 11 lands on moon - mission accomplished
1970: Apollo 13 incident; Nixon cuts NASA budget
1972: Space Shuttle approved (January 5)
1973: Apollo program ends; Skylab begins
1975: Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (final Apollo hardware)
1976: Viking lands on Mars; Shuttle development intensifies
1977: Enterprise test flights begin

APOLLO HAD A CLEAR MANDATE:
  "Land a man on the Moon and return him safely to Earth"
  - Binary success criteria
  - Declarative communication style
  - "We will do X by Y date"

SHUTTLE REQUIRED COST JUSTIFICATION:
  - No clear end goal
  - Reusable vehicle = complex lifecycle costing
  - Must justify costs to skeptical Congress annually
  - "We estimate X will cost Y and take Z months"

The shift from declarations to estimates reflects:
  - Loss of Cold War urgency post-Apollo
  - Congressional budget skepticism
  - Inherent uncertainty in reusable vehicle economics
  - Transition from "mission" to "capability" framing
